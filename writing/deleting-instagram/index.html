Deleting Instagram

Deleting social media has been one of the best decisions I’ve made in years. For me, it was Instagram. I was spending hours every day doomscrolling and numbing my brain. It’s crazy how effective these apps are. It’s been a month since I deleted it, and it has been incredibly freeing.

I’d been toying with the idea of disconnecting from social media for a while but never acted on it. Then, in mid-to-late October, I went to Turkey for my honeymoon. I’m early in my marriage, and I noticed the toll it was taking on my wife. Instead of being present and enjoying Istanbul, I was glued to my phone, mindlessly watching Reels. Enough was enough, so I pulled the trigger.

I deleted all of my Instagram accounts: my main, my fitness account, my car account, and a few others I don’t even remember anymore. Instagram makes this process annoying and difficult. They know what they’re doing. When it was finally done, I felt a weight lift off my body and soul.

The first week

The first week felt strange. I was bored, and I didn’t know what to do instead of automatically opening my phone. So I used the boredom to explore. I finally started taking Duolingo seriously and began learning Turkish. I focused on my personal projects and business ideas. I picked my dusty Kindle back up and rekindled my love of reading. Most importantly, I gave my wife the love and attention she deserves.

My anxiety went down, and I felt free. It’s not perfect. I still fall into the trap of doomscrolling YouTube. I’m thinking about how to remove that distraction too, but I use YouTube for learning. Maybe I can delete the app and use the website only to look up what I need. I’m sure I’ll figure it out.

A month in

A month into getting my attention and focus back, it’s been great. My ADHD brain is having a blast.

I finished Rework by Jason Fried and David Heinemeier Hansson, and I’m applying what I learned about working, building and growing a product. I highly recommend it to anyone who is a builder at heart.

On Duolingo, I’m at level 7 in Turkish, level 3 in Arabic, and level 10 in Russian, with a 28-day streak. I know, I know, I’m juggling three languages. Let me explain. My native tongue is Uzbek, a Turkic language similar to Turkish. A lot of the words overlap, though the spelling is annoying to get right. I was fluent in Russian before moving to the States when I was 11, so I’m brushing up on my rusty Russian. That makes Arabic the only truly new language, and I’ll be honest: it’s hard. The letters, their shapes, the pronunciation. So I rotate between the three to keep it interesting.

Building in public

I also started my personal brand and began building Hozir, DoorsAway and Ustahona. While reading and applying Rework, I realized I LOVE BUILDING THINGS. The book says to scratch your own itch and build things for yourself. That’s how Hozir was born: a simple app that tells you what to focus on right now.

Building all of these tools has also taught me that getting people to use your app is hard. Not easy at all. AI has made it easy to build software, but attracting users is the real behemoth. So I picked up Show Your Work! by Austin Kleon. It’s been great, and the main thesis of both books is the same: you need to market yourself and build your own audience. So that’s what I’m doing.

If you’ve been thinking about it

I encourage you to delete your social media. Trust me, it’s worth it. You might feel FOMO, but your close friends and family are only a call or text away to share what you’ve been missing. Do it for yourself.
